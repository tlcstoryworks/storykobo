Account, Data Export & Closure

This document is part of the evolving storykōbō policy set. It may be revised before launch and as the service changes.

Last updated: 30 October 2026 · Version 0.1 · Working draft

Read the plain-language guide · All policies

Status: Approved working draft — pre-launch operational and legal review required

Version: 0.1

Effective date: To be set before launch

1. Purpose

This policy describes the intended member choices for account access, data export, account closure, and removal of member-controlled content. The final published version must match the actual application, storage, backups, and legal obligations.

2. Account access and control

Members should be able to access and manage their account using the controls provided by the service. Account recovery and compromised-account procedures should protect the member without creating an easy route for another person to take over an account.

The service may temporarily restrict account access when reasonably necessary to protect account security, investigate serious abuse, comply with law, or maintain service integrity. Such action should be documented and handled consistently with the moderation and privacy policies.

3. Data export

Members should have a clear route to request or obtain an export of personal information and member-created content associated with their account, subject to technical feasibility, other people's rights and privacy, security, and applicable law.

The export should use a reasonably accessible, documented format. It should explain what is included and identify categories that cannot be provided, such as another person's private information, confidential moderation material, or information that must be withheld for legal or safety reasons.

The actual export scope, format, delivery method, and response timeframe must be defined and tested before launch.

4. Account closure

Members may request closure of their account. The service should explain what closure does, what happens to public contributions and collaborative material, what information is deleted or de-identified, and what may be retained.

The process should confirm the request and provide a reasonable opportunity to understand its consequences before irreversible action. Closure should not be made unnecessarily difficult or used to pressure a member to remain.

5. Member content and collaborative context

Members retain ownership of their work. The service should provide a clear choice about removal of member-controlled work when an account closes, subject to the platform's limited hosting permission, other members' rights, legal obligations, and safety or dispute needs.

Where removing a contribution would materially disrupt a shared discussion or record, the service should consider the least intrusive approach that respects the creator's rights and the privacy and legitimate interests of others. The exact treatment of replies, quotations, collaborative projects, and attribution must be defined before launch; no blanket promise of retaining or deleting all contributions is made here.

6. Retention exceptions and backups

Some information may need to be retained for legal compliance, security, fraud prevention, an unresolved safety matter, dispute handling, or legitimate service records. Retention should be limited, access restricted, and the reason documented.

Deleted information may remain in protected backups for a defined period until those backups expire or are securely rotated. The service should not restore deleted data into active use except as needed for recovery or legal/security purposes, and should reapply valid deletion requests after restoration where appropriate.

The final policy must state actual backup and retention timelines and explain exceptions accurately.

7. Requests involving another person's information

An export or deletion request does not give a member access to another person's private information or authority to delete another person's account or work. Requests affecting collaborative or shared material should be assessed with attention to ownership, permissions, privacy, and safety.

8. Identity and verification

The service should request only information reasonably necessary to confirm that the requester controls the account or is otherwise authorized. Verification should be proportionate and should not require identity documents or legal paperwork unless a specific legal or security requirement makes that necessary.

9. Contact, timelines, and readiness

Before launch, define the request route, verification steps, export format, processing timelines, closure behavior, deletion schedule, backup expiry, exceptions, and appeal or correction route for mistaken actions. These procedures must be tested against the real system. The plain-language companion is explanatory; this policy governs if they differ.
