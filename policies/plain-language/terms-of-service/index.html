This guide gives a general explanation of the Terms of Service. The formal Terms govern if anything here differs.

What the Terms cover

The Terms are the basic agreement for using storykōbō. The service is for adults aged 18 or older. You must meet that requirement and provide truthful account information.

Your account

You are responsible for activity through your account and should protect your sign-in details. Tell us through the designated contact route if you think someone has accessed your account without permission. Pen names and multiple accounts can be used, but not to impersonate people, evade restrictions, or manipulate the community.

Your writing stays yours

You keep ownership of your original work. When you choose to share something on storykōbō, you give the platform only the limited permission needed to host and show it through the features you choose. You do not hand over ownership.

The exact handling of copies, removal, and backups must match the actual service and the Creative Work and Privacy policies before launch.

Deals between members

If members make a collaboration, commission, license, or publishing agreement, that agreement is between them. storykōbō does not negotiate or guarantee those deals. We can still respond to harassment, fraud, impersonation, or other misconduct that happens through community interactions.

Community rules and moderation

Using storykōbō means following its community and safety policies. Moderators may restrict content, features, or accounts when the policies or safety of the community require it. The moderation policy explains notices and appeals.

Privacy and service availability

The Privacy Policy explains how information is handled. The service may change, need maintenance, or become temporarily unavailable. We cannot promise that moderation will prevent every harmful interaction or that the service will always work without interruption.

Important before launch

The formal Terms still need legal review for the actual operator, service, vendors, and applicable law. They also need to match what the website really does. Details such as liability, dispute resolution, and governing law have deliberately not been invented in this draft.
