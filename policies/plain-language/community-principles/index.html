This guide explains the ideas behind storykōbō's Community Principles in everyday language. It is here to make the principles easier to understand, not to replace the formal policy.

What kind of community is this?

storykōbō is being built as an 18+ creative community. You do not have to be a professional, publish your work, post every day, or prove how productive you are to belong here.

You can make things, share them, talk with people, cheer others on, browse quietly, join an event, or take a break. Those are all valid ways to participate.

Your work is yours

You keep ownership of the creative work you make.

Choosing to share something on storykōbō does not mean that the platform or another member owns it. It also does not give other members permission to copy it, repost it, adapt it, or use it commercially.

When you share something, the platform should ask only for the permissions it needs to provide the feature you're using.

There is no participation scoreboard

You do not need to compete for attention, keep up a posting streak, collect popularity, or prove that you are creating enough.

Recognition and community history can be nice. They should not become a measure of someone's worth or a reason to pressure people into constant activity.

Make room for different creative choices

People create different things, in different ways, for different reasons. storykōbō should leave room for that variety while still having clear limits on harmful behavior and prohibited content.

You can tell others what kind of feedback you want. You can also set boundaries around what you do and do not want to see.

Creative freedom does not mean ignoring someone else's boundaries.

Ask before assuming permission

Do not assume someone wants feedback, contact, collaboration, or permission to use their work.

This matters especially when sharing another person's work or putting their creative material into an outside tool or service. Respect the choices and boundaries people communicate.

Accessibility belongs here

People have different bodies, abilities, energy levels, communication styles, and technology needs.

The goal is to build accessibility and flexibility into the community rather than treating them as extras. You should not have to participate in one particular way to belong.

Your privacy matters

You should have meaningful choices about what you share and who can see it.

You should not have to disclose unnecessary personal information just to take part. Information about you, your group memberships, and your communications should be treated carefully.

Welcoming does not mean boundary-free

A healthy community needs ways to address harassment, discrimination, intimidation, exploitation, retaliation, and other harmful behavior.

Reports should be handled carefully. Members should be able to understand moderation decisions and have a meaningful way to appeal when appropriate. People who make good-faith reports or appeals should not be punished just for speaking up.

Moderation should be fair

Rules should be understandable. Decisions should consider evidence, context, and the seriousness of what happened.

Popularity, personal favoritism, disagreement, or retaliation should not decide who gets moderated. The community should keep enough records to support accountability and seek independent review when feasible.

We do not manage members' private agreements

If members decide to collaborate, commission work, license something, publish together, or enter another private business or creative arrangement, they are responsible for working out that agreement with each other.

storykōbō can address misconduct that affects its community. It is not the negotiator, guarantor, or administrator of private agreements between members.

The community will change as it grows

storykōbō is still being developed. Features and policies may change, but changes should continue to respect privacy, accessibility, creative autonomy, and community well-being.

When a policy changes, members should be told what changed and what it means for them.

What this guide is — and is not

This is an explanation, not a separate set of rules. The formal Community Principles are the authoritative version. The two documents should be kept in sync.

Some of the principles describe goals for the platform, not features that are already available. The detailed policies and actual platform practices will need to be in place before launch.
