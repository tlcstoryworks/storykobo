Community Guidelines

The formal policy and its plain-language companion are provided together so visitors can understand both the commitments and their practical meaning.

Last updated: 30 October 2026 · Version 0.1 · Working draft

Status: Companion guide to the approved working draft of the Community Guidelines

Version: 0.1

Effective date: To be set before community launch

This guide explains the everyday expectations for participating in storykōbō. It is meant to be easy to read and practical. It does not replace the formal Community Guidelines.

Treat people like people

You can disagree with someone without attacking them. Do not harass, threaten, intimidate, demean, or repeatedly target another member. Respect requests to stop a conversation or leave someone alone.

You do not have to like everyone's work or agree with every opinion. You do need to let other people participate without being targeted.

Respect boundaries

Ask before giving critique if the creator has not invited it. Respect people's preferences about contact, collaboration, feedback, and visibility.

Do not pressure someone to respond, explain themselves, work with you, or share more than they want to. Do not repost or reuse someone else's work without permission.

A public profile or project page does not mean someone has agreed to every kind of contact.

Check feedback preferences

The goal is to make feedback useful to the person receiving it.

  • Check the creator's preferences before commenting.
  • Pay attention to the settings for the specific project or piece.
  • A project's preferences take priority over the creator's general preferences.
  • If someone asks for encouragement only, do not give unsolicited critique.
  • Keep feedback relevant to what the creator asked for.

Your personal taste is not an objective measure of whether someone else's work is good.

Share work you have permission to share

Share your own work or work you have permission to post. Give credit where it is appropriate. Do not claim someone else's work as yours.

storykōbō is intended for excerpts and project progress, not hosting complete manuscripts. Creators can link to full-length work hosted elsewhere.

Being able to view something online does not mean you have permission to copy or reuse it.

Label content honestly

Use the required content labels and warnings. Do not use misleading titles, tags, previews, or descriptions to get around content settings.

Respect other members' filters and preferences. Do not deliberately put material in front of someone who has chosen to exclude it.

Make room for creative differences

People have different interests, styles, genres, and approaches. You can decide that a particular work is not for you without attacking its creator.

Creative freedom does not excuse harassment, threats, exploitation, or other prohibited behavior.

Be honest about authorship and tools

Do not claim another person's work as yours or misrepresent your contribution to a collaboration.

Do not put another member's creative work into an AI system or outside processing service without their explicit permission. Follow the platform's separate rules about AI-generated media and assistive tools.

Use community features in good faith

Do not spam, impersonate people to deceive or harm them, manipulate community systems, or misuse reporting and appeals to target someone.

Multiple accounts, pseudonyms, and separate creative identities are allowed in themselves. The issue is deceptive or harmful behavior, not simply having more than one account.

Disagree without escalating

Not every disagreement is a policy violation. When possible, focus on the issue, avoid personal attacks, and step away if a conversation stops being productive.

Respect blocking and other boundaries. You do not have to work things out directly with someone who has harassed, threatened, or harmed you. Use the reporting process when you have a genuine safety or policy concern.

Follow the rules for each space and event

Groups, events, challenges, and collaborative activities may have additional rules. Follow the expectations posted for the space.

Events are voluntary. Deadlines and goals should not be used to shame people for illness, disability, caregiving, limited time, or changing circumstances.

Protect privacy and safety

Do not publish another member's private information, stalk or monitor them, or try to access accounts or spaces you are not allowed to use.

Do not try to identify, locate, or contact someone outside the platform in ways they have not welcomed. Use the appropriate reporting channel for serious safety concerns.

Report honestly and respect the process

If you report something, describe what you experienced or directly observed as accurately as you can. Do not knowingly fabricate evidence or use reports to punish someone for a disagreement.

Good-faith reports are still good-faith reports even if moderators cannot confirm a violation. Do not retaliate against someone for reporting or appealing.

Other policies explain the details

These guidelines work alongside the platform's other policies, including Acceptable Use, Safety and Moderation, Creative Work and Copyright, AI Use, Privacy, Accessibility, Events, and Account and Data policies.

A behavior can raise more than one policy concern. The moderation process should consider the situation as a whole and avoid unnecessary duplicate investigations or penalties.

What this guide is — and is not

This is an everyday explanation, not a separate set of rules. The formal Community Guidelines are authoritative. The two documents should be kept aligned.

Some referenced features and procedures may not yet be implemented. The relevant policies and actual platform capabilities must be ready before launch.
