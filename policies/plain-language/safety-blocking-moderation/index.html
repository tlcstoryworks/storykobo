Safety, Blocking & Moderation Policy

The formal policy and its plain-language companion are provided together so visitors can understand both the commitments and their practical meaning.

The formal Safety, Blocking & Moderation Policy is the governing document. This guide explains what it means in everyday use.

Blocking

Blocking is quiet. The other account is not notified that you blocked it. A block applies across your account, not just one conversation or project. It is meant to stop direct and indirect interaction, including attempts to reach you through another member-facing route.

Both accounts may still be able to view shared community spaces. Blocking cannot promise that you will never see something the other person posts in a shared space. Blocking is a personal boundary, not proof that someone broke a rule.

Reporting

Members can report something they experienced or directly witnessed. Reports are anonymous to the person being reported and to ordinary members, but moderators and authorized staff handling the case can see who submitted them.

You should receive confirmation that a report arrived. Where the system allows, you should also receive a status and closure notice. We may not be able to share private details about another member or every step taken.

Please do not put yourself at risk to gather evidence or investigate. Good-faith reports are protected even when the moderators cannot confirm them.

How moderators respond

Reports are assessed for urgency, evidence, context, and possible harm. If waiting would create serious risk, one moderator may take urgent protective action. The action should be documented and independently checked when feasible.

Moderators should disclose conflicts and step aside when needed. Significant decisions should receive an independent review when possible. If that cannot happen, we should be honest about the limitation.

Decisions and appeals

Possible responses range from guidance or a warning to content restrictions, feature limits, suspension, or account removal. The response should fit the situation.

When it is safe and appropriate, the affected member should be told what policy was involved and given enough information to understand the decision. Members can appeal formal restrictions, suspensions, and removal. An appeal does not automatically reverse or pause a safety measure.

We do not tolerate retaliation against someone for reporting or appealing in good faith. Permanent removal should have a reconsideration route unless safety or legal reasons prevent it.

Records and limits

We should keep only the case information reasonably needed, restrict who can see it, and establish retention and deletion rules before launch.

Moderators are not emergency services, law enforcement, therapists, or legal advisers. If you are in immediate danger, contact appropriate local emergency or support services.

The tools and procedures described here must actually be ready before launch. This guide will be kept in sync with the formal policy.
