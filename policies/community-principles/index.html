Community Principles

The formal policy and its plain-language companion are provided together so visitors can understand both the commitments and their practical meaning.

Last updated: 30 October 2026 · Version 0.1 · Working draft

Read the plain-language guide · Back to all policies

Status: Approved working draft; pre-launch review remains ongoing

Version: 0.1

Effective date: To be set before community launch

Applies to: storykōbō community design, participation, and governance

These principles describe the values that guide storykōbō. They are the foundation for the more specific community rules and operational policies. They should be read alongside those policies, which explain how these commitments work in practice.

1. A community for adults

storykōbō is an 18+ creative community. Its spaces, expectations, and community systems are designed around adult participation.

Membership does not require a particular profession, level of experience, publication history, creative output, or degree of public participation.

2. Creativity belongs to its creator

Members retain ownership of the original creative work they make.

Sharing work on storykōbō does not transfer ownership to the platform or grant other members permission to reproduce, distribute, adapt, or commercially exploit it.

Members should be able to choose what they share, how they share it, and what permissions they grant. The platform should request only the permissions reasonably necessary to provide the features a member chooses to use.

3. Participation is a choice, not a performance

There is no single correct way to participate.

Members may create, share, discuss, encourage, browse quietly, participate in events, or take breaks. None of these choices makes someone more or less deserving of belonging.

The community should not turn participation into a competition for visibility, productivity, popularity, or attention.

4. Respect creative autonomy

Members have different creative interests, processes, experiences, and comfort levels.

The community should make room for a broad range of creative expression while maintaining clear boundaries around harmful conduct and prohibited material.

Members should be able to communicate their preferences, including what kind of feedback they welcome and what content they do not wish to encounter.

Creative freedom does not remove the responsibility to respect other members.

5. Consent and boundaries matter

Permission should not be assumed.

This applies to sharing another person's work, giving feedback, contacting members, involving someone in a collaboration, and using another member's creative work with external tools or services.

Members should respect stated boundaries and should not use community access to pressure, harass, exploit, or retaliate against others.

6. Accessibility is part of the foundation

Accessibility is not an optional enhancement or a special accommodation reserved for a few members.

storykōbō should work toward an environment that accommodates different bodies, minds, abilities, communication styles, energy levels, and ways of interacting with technology.

Customization and flexible participation should support that goal.

7. Privacy deserves active protection

Members should have meaningful control over what information they share and who can see it.

The platform should avoid making personal disclosure a requirement for ordinary participation. Private information, contextual group memberships, and member communications should be handled with care.

Privacy should be considered when designing features, not addressed only after problems arise.

8. Community care includes accountability

A welcoming community still needs boundaries.

Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, exploitation, retaliation, and other harmful conduct should be addressed through clear and proportionate community processes.

Reports should be handled with appropriate confidentiality and care. Members should have access to understandable decisions and a meaningful opportunity to appeal moderation actions.

Good-faith reporting and appeals should be protected.

9. Moderation should be fair, consistent, and accountable

Community rules should be understandable and applied with attention to context, evidence, and the seriousness of the conduct.

Moderation should not be based on popularity, personal favoritism, disagreement, or retaliation.

The platform should provide notice and an appeal process where appropriate, maintain records sufficient for accountability, and seek independent review when feasible.

10. Community membership is not a private contract service

storykōbō governs participation in its own community. It does not govern the private creative or business relationships between members.

Members remain responsible for negotiating and managing their own collaborations, commissions, licensing arrangements, publishing agreements, and other independent partnerships.

The platform may address misconduct that occurs through community interactions, but it does not act as an intermediary, guarantor, or administrator of private agreements between members.

11. The community should grow without losing its purpose

Features, policies, and community practices will evolve as storykōbō develops.

Changes should be considered in light of the principles above. Growth should not require abandoning privacy, accessibility, creative autonomy, or a healthy community environment.

When policies change, members should be given clear information about what changed and what those changes mean for participation.

How these principles relate to other policies

This document establishes foundational values. It does not replace the detailed rules and procedures in the other community policies. Those documents explain how the principles are applied to specific situations.

If a plain-language companion guide and a formal policy appear to conflict, the formal policy is the controlling document. The two should be kept aligned and updated together.

This is a working policy document for a platform in development. It is not a statement that every described feature or process is currently implemented or available. Operational commitments should be reflected in the applicable policies and in the platform's actual practices before launch.
