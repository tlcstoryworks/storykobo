POLICY LIBRARY

Events & Challenges

Events are invitations to make things together, meet people, and encourage one another. They should never become a test of worth or productivity.

1. Voluntary participation

Members may join, pause, change their level of participation, or leave without shame or penalty. No one should be pressured to explain illness, disability, caregiving, limited time, missed goals, or absence.

2. Teams are for belonging, not rivalry

Each participant may belong to one team per event. A member may join a different team in another event. Teams may have names, symbols, colors, mottos, banners, and team discussion spaces. Team membership does not imply project ownership or collaboration.

Teams exist to create shared identity and encouragement. They are not opponents. Events must not reward defeating another team, rank teams by output, or use team totals to shame members.

3. Flexible goals and progress

Goals should support different creative disciplines and personal circumstances. Measures may include words, scenes, chapters, sessions, minutes, revision passes, practice time, or qualitative milestones. A numerical goal is not required for participation.

Progress sharing is optional. Members should be able to choose whether updates are private, visible to their team, or shared with the wider community. Progress records must not become public productivity rankings or popularity metrics.

4. Accessibility and privacy

Organizers should consider schedules, time zones, energy levels, devices, access needs, and communication preferences. Asynchronous participation, flexible pacing, alternatives to public posting, and breaks should be supported where practical. No one should have to disclose private medical information to take part or ask for an adjustment.

5. Recognition and keepsakes

Recognition should celebrate personal milestones and collective accomplishments without implying that nonparticipants or people with different goals are less valued. Personal awards, event keepsakes, team pennants, and community milestones should not be ranked by rarity, output, or popularity.

Personal event banners may preserve the event theme and a participant's team identity. The side ribbon may show the team's symbol, name, and year; earned pixel-art keepsakes may decorate the banner field. Collective pennants represent shared team or community accomplishments.

6. Events and creative work

Events use storykōbō's community features. They do not create an exception to the no-hosting boundary. Actual creative works remain on external services chosen by their creators; event participation may use project descriptions, progress notes, discussion, and external links.

7. Clear event information

Organizers should explain an event's purpose, dates, time zones, eligibility, activities, expectations, content boundaries, and how to raise concerns before it begins. Material changes, delays, or cancellations should be communicated clearly.

8. Working status

This policy is a working draft and must be checked against implemented event, visibility, moderation, and accessibility features before launch.
