The bigger picture

A place to make things, and room to be a person.

We want the internet to have more places where creative people can gather, work quietly, share generously, and feel part of something.

The third-place idea

A third place sits beyond home and work: a library, café, workshop, club, or community space where people can spend time without needing to justify every minute. These spaces help people meet, learn, and belong.

Online, so much of our time is organized around feeds, metrics, advertising, and the pressure to stay visible. storykōbō is being designed around a different question: what would a creative online place feel like if belonging mattered more than attention?

A library, a workshop, a commons

The library

A place to discover work, follow curiosity, read, and find resources — including things that are not currently trending.

The workshop

A place to develop creative projects, document progress, experiment, and share the work at different stages of becoming.

The commons

A place for discussion, groups, events, mutual encouragement, and the everyday exchanges that make a community feel alive.

Creative work is bigger than one medium

Writing is an important starting point, but creativity takes many forms: fiction, poetry, worldbuilding, comics, game writing, zines, visual storytelling, nonfiction, collaborative projects, and practices that don't fit neatly into a category. The platform should make room for different ways of working rather than requiring everyone to create the same way.

Community over competition

Participation should not become a public ranking system. We want to avoid building the experience around popularity scores, productivity comparisons, engagement farming, or pressure to constantly perform. Recognition and community history can still matter without turning people into a leaderboard.

Built to grow thoughtfully

The long-term vision includes native accounts and profiles, creative projects, groups, forums, activity, events, discovery tools, achievements, personalization, and optional ways to support the project. These are ambitions, not promises that every feature is already available.

We’ll build in stages, keep the foundations understandable, and make room to learn as the community takes shape.

A principle to keep

Come as you are.

Quiet browsing is participation. So is sharing work, joining a conversation, helping someone along, or simply spending time in a place that feels welcoming.
