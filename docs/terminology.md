# storykōbō terminology

This document defines the working meaning of major platform concepts so that different systems do not accidentally become the same thing under different names.

## Account

The authentication identity used to sign in.

## User

The person represented by an account.

Every user has a permanent internal numeric ID.

## Username

The public, claimable name used in a profile URL such as `/@username`.

## Profile

The public-facing representation of a user.

## Role

A permission or responsibility designation.

Examples may eventually include member, moderator, librarian, staff, or administrator.

A role answers:

> What can this person do?

## Rank

A user's progression or community standing.

Rank should not determine every permission.

A rank answers:

> Where is this person in the community's progression system?

## Group

A collection of users who share some membership, context, interest, or affiliation.

Examples may include:

- genre
- region
- interest
- age range
- event team
- sponsor/supporter status

Group visibility and membership rules may differ by group.

## Team

A group with a specific collaborative or event-oriented purpose.

Teams may be temporary.

## Achievement

A record of something a user has accomplished, participated in, or unlocked.

Achievements are separate from activity.

## Flair

A visual identifier associated with a user.

Flair may communicate identity, interests, achievements, teams, or other community-recognized information.

## Activity

A record of an action or event involving a user or community object.

Activity is primarily chronological and contextual.

## Project

A creative work or body of work represented on the platform.

A project may have its own updates, participants, activity, and visibility settings.

## Event

An organized community activity with a defined participation period, rules, or purpose.

Events may contain teams, goals, achievements, and activity.

## Forum

The discussion component of storykōbō.

The forum is a native part of the platform rather than a separate identity system.

## Cheer

A lightweight positive acknowledgement.

A cheer is not intended to function as karma, currency, or a public popularity score.
