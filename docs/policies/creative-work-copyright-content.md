# Creative Work, Copyright & Content Policy

**Status:** Approved working draft — pre-launch legal and operational review required  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Effective date:** To be set before launch

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how creative work may be shared on storykōbō, how ownership and permissions work, and how content boundaries, attribution, and copyright concerns are handled.

## 2. What members may share

storykōbō is designed for excerpts, project progress, discussion, and creative community activity—not as a repository for complete manuscripts or full-length works. Members may share excerpts within applicable limits and link to external places where they choose to host complete work.

Members are responsible for ensuring they have the rights and permissions needed to share material. Do not post another person's work, private correspondence, commissioned material, or collaborative material beyond the permission you have.

## 3. Ownership and platform permission

Members retain ownership of original creative work. By choosing to post material, a member grants the platform only the limited permission needed to host, store, format, and display that material through the features the member chooses and as necessary to operate the service. This is not a transfer of ownership and is not a general license for unrelated reuse, sale, publication, or AI training.

Attribution and permission are separate. Giving credit does not create permission to copy, adapt, publish, or otherwise use another person's work.

## 4. Attribution and collaboration

Credit contributors accurately and respect agreed contribution terms. A member's presence in a collaboration does not automatically grant permission to reuse every contribution elsewhere. Members should make private agreements directly with one another.

storykōbō does not negotiate, guarantee, administer, or enforce private creative or business agreements. It may address community misconduct, including harassment, fraud, or impersonation, connected to those interactions.

## 5. Content labels and maturity

Content labels should help members make informed choices without requiring creators to disclose more than necessary. The planned system distinguishes:
- Ordinary descriptive tags.
- Mandatory content warnings for specified themes or material.
- Maturity-triggering tags that affect visibility and access.
- Prohibited content that cannot be shared.

The planned discovery model uses opt-in preferences and include/exclude tag controls. Members may set project-level defaults and individual restrictions. Individual preferences do not override platform rules, access controls, or prohibited-content boundaries.

Where content is not opted into by a viewer, the intended presentation is a neutral placeholder with a safe title and no excerpt or thumbnail that reveals the restricted material. The exact maturity categories, mandatory warnings, prohibited categories, and age/access rules must be finalized and implemented before launch.

## 6. Fiction, fandom, and difficult themes

The community aims to welcome original fiction and creative exploration without treating difficult themes as automatically unacceptable. Context, framing, purpose, and presentation matter. Fictional depiction is not automatically endorsement.

Some themes may appear in fandom-specific writing or in work tied to particular historical or cultural periods. Such context does not create an automatic exemption from content rules, warnings, or safety boundaries. The community's outreach emphasis on original fiction is not a prohibition on all fan works; individual works are assessed under the same applicable rules.

## 7. Prohibited content and boundaries

Content that falls within the platform's prohibited categories may not be posted, regardless of tags or viewer preferences. No tag, project setting, or opt-in choice can bypass platform-level restrictions. The detailed prohibited categories must be stated clearly in the launch version and applied consistently with the Acceptable Use Policy and Community Guidelines.

## 8. Copyright concerns and reports

A person who believes their rights have been infringed should be able to submit a report through a designated route. The process should request enough information to identify the material and explain the claimed rights, while limiting unnecessary personal information.

Reports should be handled confidentially and shared only with people who need them to assess the claim. Reporter identity should not be disclosed to the reported member except where required by law or necessary process, and any such disclosure should be explained where legally permitted.

The platform may restrict access to or remove disputed material while assessing a report. A report is not automatically proof of infringement. The process must include a way for affected members to respond and, where appropriate, appeal or request reconsideration.

## 9. Removal requests and exceptions

Creators may request removal of their own work or material they control. The platform should explain the request process and act within a reasonable, stated timeframe once implemented.

Removal may be limited where material must be retained for legal compliance, an active safety or abuse investigation, dispute resolution, security, or protected backups. Any exception should be narrow, documented, and explained when possible. Removal of a public copy may not erase copies independently made or hosted elsewhere.

## 10. Repeat infringement and enforcement

Repeated or knowing infringement may lead to graduated action, taking account of severity, evidence, intent, response, and prior relevant findings. Responses may include education, content restriction or removal, warnings, feature limits, suspension, or account removal. Good-faith mistakes should be distinguished from deliberate or repeated abuse.

## 11. Readiness and related policies

The final policy must define excerpt limits, prohibited categories, maturity labels, reporting details, response timelines, and the operating contact before launch. It must match actual technical access controls and removal capabilities. Read this with the Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Acceptable Use Policy, Community Guidelines, and AI Use & Human Authorship Policy. The plain-language companion is explanatory; this policy governs if they differ.
