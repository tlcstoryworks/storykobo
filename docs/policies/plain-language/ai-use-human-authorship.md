# AI Use & Human Authorship: The Plain-Language Guide

**Status:** Approved working draft — pre-launch review required  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Effective date:** To be set before launch

The formal AI Use & Human Authorship Policy governs if anything here differs.

## Human authorship matters

storykōbō is built around human creative work. You are responsible for what you submit and should represent your contribution honestly.

## Assistive tools are different from generated creative work

You may use tools to help brainstorm, organize, outline, check spelling or grammar, support accessibility, research, or translate while you remain responsible for the creative decisions and final work.

AI-generated text, images, audio, and other media are not permitted as creative submissions in storykōbō creative spaces under the current policy direction. Any narrow exception would need to be clearly defined before launch.

If an event or collaboration has a specific disclosure rule, follow it. Don't present substantially AI-generated work as entirely human-created when that would mislead people.

## Do not put someone else's work into an AI tool without permission

You need the creator's explicit permission before giving their writing, private messages, personal information, or unpublished work to an AI system or outside processing service. This includes asking a tool to critique, summarize, edit, translate, or train on it.

A story being visible on storykōbō does not mean its creator agreed to external AI processing.

## The platform and AI training

storykōbō does not claim a general right to train AI models on member work. Scraping or collecting member work for training without permission is not allowed. A future AI feature that processes member content would need clear advance notice and an appropriate opt-in.

## Keep concerns respectful

Disclose assistance when the rules require it or when leaving it out would materially mislead others. Do not use AI accusations to shame or harass another member. Raise concerns through the appropriate process.

Event and collaboration rules may add clear requirements, but they cannot override the platform-wide restrictions.
