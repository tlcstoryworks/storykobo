# Events & Challenges: The Plain-Language Guide

**Status:** Approved working draft — pre-launch review required  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Effective date:** To be set before launch

The formal Events & Challenges Policy is the governing document.

## Events are invitations, not obligations

Events and challenges are meant to help people connect and create. Joining is optional. You can pause, participate less, or leave without having to explain why. Illness, disability, caregiving, limited time, and missed goals are not reasons to shame anyone.

We do not want leaderboards, public productivity rankings, or streaks that turn writing into a competition. Recognition should not decide who belongs.

## Clear expectations

Organizers should explain the dates, time zones, activities, rules, content boundaries, and any judging before the event starts. If rules change or an event is cancelled, organizers should communicate that as clearly and quickly as they can.

## Make room for different ways to participate

Events should consider different schedules, energy levels, access needs, devices, and communication styles. Asynchronous participation, flexible pacing, breaks, and alternatives to public posting should be considered where practical.

You should not have to disclose private medical information to take part or ask for an adjustment.

## Respect creative boundaries

Being part of an event does not mean you have agreed to receive critique. Your feedback preferences still matter. Event-specific AI, authorship, originality, or disclosure rules must be stated in advance and cannot override platform-wide policies.

If an event has judging or prizes, the criteria and decision process should be clear. Not winning—or not participating—does not make someone less valued.
