# Community Guidelines

**Status:** Approved working draft; pre-launch review remains ongoing  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Effective date:** To be set before community launch  
**Applies to:** Member participation and interactions in storykōbō community spaces

These guidelines explain how members are expected to treat one another and participate in storykōbō. They apply to community spaces and interactions, including discussions, project pages, comments, groups, events, and direct interactions made through the platform.

## 1. Treat other members with respect

- Speak to other members as people, not as audiences, targets, or obstacles.
- Disagree with ideas without attacking the person expressing them.
- Do not harass, threaten, intimidate, demean, or repeatedly target another member.
- Do not use discrimination, slurs, or identity-based hostility to exclude or mistreat people.
- Respect requests to stop a conversation or leave someone alone.

You do not have to agree with everyone. You do have to respect their right to participate without being targeted.

## 2. Respect boundaries and consent

- Ask before giving critique when the creator has not already invited it.
- Respect a member's stated preferences about contact, feedback, collaboration, and visibility.
- Do not pressure someone to respond, explain themselves, collaborate, or share more than they wish.
- Do not repost, redistribute, adapt, or otherwise use another member's work without the necessary permission.
- Do not share private communications or personal information without permission, except where disclosure is necessary for a legitimate safety or moderation report.

A public profile or project page is not an invitation to disregard someone's boundaries.

## 3. Follow feedback and critique preferences

Feedback should be useful to the person receiving it, not simply delivered because someone wants to give it.

storykōbō's intended feedback model is based on asking first and allowing creators to choose the kind of response they welcome.

Members should:

- Check a creator's feedback preferences before commenting on their work.
- Respect the preferences attached to the specific project or piece.
- Treat work-specific preferences as taking priority over a member's general feedback preferences.
- Avoid unsolicited critique when a creator has requested encouragement only or has not invited critique.
- Keep feedback relevant to the work and the kind of response requested.

A difference in taste is not automatically a flaw. Critique should not be used as a pretext for ridicule, hostility, or control over another person's creative choices.

## 4. Share creative work responsibly

Members decide what they are ready to share.

- Share only work you own or have permission to share.
- Credit other creators where attribution is appropriate.
- Do not present another person's work as your own.
- Do not upload full manuscripts or complete works where the platform is intended for excerpts and project progress.
- Use external links for full-length writing hosted elsewhere, while respecting the rights and preferences of the creator.
- Do not assume that something being publicly viewable makes it free to reuse.

The platform's Creative Work, Copyright & Content policy will explain ownership, permissions, attribution, reporting, and content-specific restrictions in more detail.

## 5. Label content thoughtfully

Members should use the platform's content labels and warnings so others can make informed choices about what they encounter.

- Apply required warnings and maturity-related tags accurately.
- Do not use misleading titles, tags, previews, or descriptions to bypass content restrictions.
- Respect other members' content preferences and filtering choices.
- Do not attempt to expose members to material they have chosen to exclude.
- Keep titles, thumbnails, excerpts, and previews appropriate for the visibility settings of the content.

The detailed content taxonomy and display rules will be defined in the Creative Work, Copyright & Content policy.

## 6. Respect creative differences

storykōbō is intended to make room for many creative interests, styles, genres, and approaches.

Members should not use disagreement about genre, subject matter, technique, or personal taste as an excuse to harass or belittle another creator.

At the same time, creative expression does not excuse targeted harassment, threats, exploitation, or other prohibited conduct.

Members may choose not to read, follow, discuss, or engage with work that is not for them. They should use available content controls and boundaries rather than targeting its creator.

## 7. Be honest about authorship and tools

Members should represent their creative work honestly.

- Do not claim another person's work as your own.
- Do not misrepresent the extent of your contribution to collaborative work.
- Do not submit another member's creative work to an AI system or external processing service without that creator's explicit permission.
- Follow the platform's rules for AI-generated media and assistive tools.

The separate AI Use & Human Authorship policy will explain the platform's boundaries around generated material, assistive uses, and disclosure. These guidelines do not create permission to use AI-generated media in creative spaces.

## 8. Participate in good faith

Members should use community features for their intended purposes.

- Do not spam, flood, or repeatedly post unwanted promotional material.
- Do not impersonate another person or misrepresent your identity in order to deceive or harm.
- Do not manipulate community systems to manufacture attention, influence, or unfair advantage.
- Do not misuse reporting, appeals, or moderation processes to target another member.
- Do not retaliate against someone for setting a boundary, making a good-faith report, or participating in an appeal.

Multiple accounts, pseudonyms, and separate creative identities are not inherently misconduct. The concern is deceptive or harmful use, not the mere existence of more than one account.

## 9. Handle disagreements constructively

Disagreements are part of community life. Not every conflict is a policy violation, and not every disagreement needs moderator involvement.

When possible:

- Address the specific issue rather than attacking someone's character.
- Avoid escalating a disagreement through repeated messages or public callouts.
- Step away when a conversation is no longer productive.
- Respect blocking and other boundaries.
- Use the reporting process when conduct crosses a community boundary or creates a safety concern.

Members are not required to resolve conflicts directly with someone who has harassed, threatened, or otherwise harmed them.

## 10. Respect community spaces and events

Different spaces may have additional expectations based on their purpose.

Members should follow the posted rules for groups, events, challenges, and collaborative activities.

Event participation should remain voluntary. Deadlines, goals, and challenges should not be used to shame members for disability, illness, caregiving responsibilities, limited time, or changes in circumstances.

Organizers may establish additional event-specific expectations, provided they do not override platform policies or members' basic rights and protections.

## 11. Respect privacy and safety

- Do not publish another member's private information.
- Do not attempt to identify, locate, or contact someone outside the platform in ways they have not welcomed.
- Do not use community access to stalk, intimidate, threaten, or monitor another member.
- Do not attempt to access accounts, private content, or restricted spaces without authorization.
- Report serious safety concerns through the appropriate reporting channel rather than escalating them publicly.

## 12. Use reporting and appeals responsibly

Members should use reporting tools for genuine concerns about conduct or content that may violate community policies.

- Report what you experienced or directly observed.
- Be as accurate and specific as you reasonably can.
- Do not knowingly fabricate evidence or make reports to punish someone for disagreement.
- Do not discourage others from making good-faith reports.
- Respect the confidentiality of moderation and appeal processes.
- Do not retaliate against anyone involved in a report or appeal.

A report does not need to result in a policy finding to have been made in good faith. Members should not be punished simply because a concern cannot be substantiated.

## 13. These guidelines work together with other policies

These guidelines are part of a larger policy framework. Other documents provide more detailed requirements for:

- Acceptable use and prohibited activity
- Safety, blocking, moderation, and appeals
- Creative work, copyright, and content
- AI use and human authorship
- Privacy and personal information
- Accessibility and participation
- Events and organized activities
- Accounts and data

A behavior may be addressed under more than one policy when it creates multiple kinds of harm. Moderation should still consider the incident as a whole and avoid unnecessary duplicate investigations or penalties.

---

*These guidelines are a working policy document for a platform in development. Some referenced features and procedures may not yet be implemented. The applicable detailed policies and actual platform capabilities must be in place before launch.*
