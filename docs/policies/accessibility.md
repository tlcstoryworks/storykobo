# Accessibility Policy

**Status:** Approved working draft — pre-launch operational and legal review required  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Effective date:** To be set before launch

## 1. Commitment

Accessibility is a foundational part of storykōbō's community design, not an optional enhancement. The service aims to support participation by people with different disabilities, access needs, devices, communication styles, energy levels, and circumstances.

## 2. Design and development

The service should be designed and tested with attention to:
- Semantic structure, keyboard access, visible focus, and predictable navigation.
- Screen-reader compatibility, meaningful labels, headings, and alternative text.
- Readable typography, sufficient contrast, zoom and reflow, and reduced-motion preferences.
- Clear language, understandable errors, and consistent interaction patterns.
- Responsive layouts and support for a range of devices and input methods.
- Avoiding unnecessary time limits, motion, sensory overload, or interaction demands.

Accessibility testing should include automated checks and manual review. Automated conformance tools alone are not sufficient.

## 3. Participation and community expectations

Members must not shame, mock, or penalize others for disability, illness, neurodivergence, caregiving, limited energy, assistive technology use, or participation at a different pace. Events and challenges should avoid making constant availability, speed, word count, public performance, or physical ability a condition of belonging unless an activity's nature genuinely requires it and an accessible alternative is considered.

No member is required to disclose a diagnosis or provide medical proof to request a reasonable participation adjustment within the community.

## 4. Content accessibility

Members are encouraged to provide useful alt text, captions, transcripts, descriptive links, and readable formatting when sharing media. The platform should support accessible content practices and avoid making inaccessible media the only way to obtain essential information.

The final implementation should define which fields or formats are required or supported and provide guidance without placing unreasonable burdens on members.

## 5. Requests and feedback

Members should have a clear way to report an accessibility barrier or request an adjustment. Requests should be considered respectfully and individually, with reasonable communication about what can be done, alternatives, and limitations. The service should not require unnecessary medical details.

Accessibility feedback should be treated as useful product information, not as an attack on the project or its members.

## 6. Events and challenges

Event organizers should consider different energy levels, time zones, communication preferences, sensory needs, and access methods. Participation should be voluntary, and alternate ways to take part should be considered where practical. Details are addressed in the Events & Challenges Policy.

## 7. Known limitations and continuous improvement

Accessibility is ongoing work. The service should publish known limitations when they materially affect participation and provide a route for feedback. We will prioritize barriers by severity, frequency, impact, and available resources, and avoid claiming full accessibility or formal conformance without appropriate testing.

## 8. Readiness

Before launch, the team must test core user journeys with keyboard and assistive technologies, check contrast and responsive behavior, provide a working accessibility contact route, and establish a process for tracking and addressing reported barriers. The plain-language companion is explanatory; this policy governs if they differ.
