# Safety, Blocking & Moderation Policy

**Status:** Approved working draft — pre-launch review required  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Effective date:** To be set before launch

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how members can manage unwanted contact, report concerns, and understand moderation decisions. The goal is a community that is safe and welcoming without promising that every conflict can be prevented or every report can be substantiated.

## 2. Blocking and personal boundaries

Members may use blocking to end unwanted interaction. Blocking is quiet: the blocked account is not told that another member blocked them. Blocking is account-wide rather than limited to one project or conversation.

A block is intended to prevent direct and indirect interaction between the accounts. Blocked accounts should not be able to contact one another, tag or target one another, or use another member-facing route to circumvent the boundary. Shared community spaces may remain accessible to both people; blocking is not a guarantee that either person will never encounter material posted in a shared space.

Blocking is a personal boundary tool, not a moderation finding. It does not itself establish that the other member violated a policy. Members should not use alternate accounts or intermediaries to get around another person's boundary.

## 3. Reporting

Reporting is available to members. A report should describe the reporter's own experience or conduct they directly observed, rather than rumor or speculation. Include relevant context and available evidence, but do not investigate, confront, or collect information in ways that put anyone at risk.

Reports are anonymous to the reported member and other ordinary members, but not to moderators or authorized staff handling the case. The platform should limit access to report information to people who need it for review and retain only the information reasonably needed for safety, accountability, appeals, and applicable obligations.

A receipt should confirm that a report was received. Where feasible, the reporter should be able to see a meaningful status and receive notice when the matter is closed. Privacy and safety may limit what can be shared about another member or the outcome.

## 4. Triage and urgent action

Reports are assessed for urgency, credibility, scope, potential harm, and available evidence. Immediate threats, active exploitation, credible risk of serious harm, account compromise, or ongoing exposure may require prompt protective action.

One moderator may take urgent action when waiting for a second reviewer would create unacceptable risk. The action and rationale should be documented, and a secondary review should occur when feasible. Urgent action is protective, not a presumption that every allegation has already been finally established.

## 5. Fair review

Moderators should consider the available evidence, context, impact, applicable policies, prior relevant findings, and the needs of affected members. Members should not be penalized merely because a report was made against them. Good-faith reports and appeals are protected even when unsubstantiated.

Moderators should disclose conflicts and recuse themselves when impartiality may reasonably be questioned. A founder or lead reviewer should independently review significant decisions where feasible. If no independent reviewer is available, that limitation should be acknowledged rather than implying independence that did not occur.

## 6. Notice and response

When reasonably safe and appropriate, a member subject to a formal restriction or sanction should receive notice of the relevant policy basis and enough information to understand the decision and respond. Notice may be limited or delayed when disclosure could expose a reporter, compromise an investigation, create a safety risk, or conflict with legal obligations.

## 7. Moderation responses

Responses should be proportionate and context-sensitive. Depending on severity, pattern, risk, and impact, they may include guidance, a request to change behavior, content removal or restriction, feature limitations, a warning, temporary suspension, or account removal.

The platform may take temporary protective action while a serious concern is reviewed. Permanent removal should have a route for reconsideration except where safety or legal constraints make reconsideration inappropriate or impossible.

## 8. Appeals

Members may appeal formal restrictions, suspensions, and removal decisions. An appeal should identify the decision and explain what the member believes was mistaken, incomplete, or disproportionate. Appeals should be reviewed by someone not responsible for the original decision where feasible. If that is not possible, the limitation should be disclosed.

Appeals do not guarantee reversal and do not automatically pause a safety measure. Retaliation against someone for making or participating in a good-faith report or appeal is prohibited.

## 9. Case records and confidentiality

Records should be limited to what is reasonably necessary to document the report, evidence considered, actions, rationale, notices, and appeal. Access should be restricted. Retention periods and deletion practices should be defined before launch and aligned with the Privacy Policy and legal obligations. Reports should not become a general-purpose archive of member activity.

## 10. Overlapping concerns

A single incident may involve multiple policies. Where appropriate, moderators should coordinate the review and maintain one coherent case record rather than duplicate investigations or penalties. Separate incidents or harms may be handled separately.

## 11. Limits

Moderation is not emergency response, law enforcement, legal advice, therapy, or a guarantee of safety. Members facing immediate danger should contact appropriate local emergency or support services. The platform may preserve or disclose information when required or permitted by applicable law, as described in the Privacy Policy.

## 12. Readiness and changes

This policy describes intended commitments. Reporting, blocking, case management, notices, appeals, access controls, and retention practices must be implemented and tested before launch. Effective date to be set before launch. The plain-language guide is explanatory; this policy governs if they differ.
