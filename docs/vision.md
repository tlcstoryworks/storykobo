# storykōbō vision

## What is storykōbō?

storykōbō is a creative community platform designed around the idea of an online third place: somewhere people can come to make things, work quietly, discover other people's work, exchange ideas, and participate in community without everything becoming a popularity contest.

The platform is intended to bring together pieces that have historically been scattered across forums, social platforms, event sites, writing communities, chat servers, and personal project pages.

## Who is it for?

storykōbō is intended for adults **18 and older** who want a place to participate in creative communities.

Writing is an important starting point, but the platform should be able to accommodate many forms of creative work, including:

- fiction
- poetry
- worldbuilding
- comics
- visual storytelling
- game writing
- zines
- nonfiction
- collaborative projects
- other creative practices

The platform should not require everyone to create in the same way.

## The third-place idea

The desired feeling is closer to a library, workshop, archive, or community commons than a conventional social-media feed.

Possible spaces and functions include:

- **Library** — discovery, reading, reference, and quiet browsing
- **Workshop** — making and developing creative projects
- **Commons** — conversation and community
- **Archive** — preserving projects, events, and community history

This is a conceptual model rather than a requirement that the interface literally use these names.

## Community philosophy

storykōbō should encourage participation without turning participation into a competition.

That means avoiding systems whose primary purpose is:

- public popularity ranking
- productivity comparison
- winner/loser hierarchies
- engagement farming
- pressure to constantly perform
- monetary status displays

Recognition can still exist. Achievements, supporter marks, event participation, teams, and other forms of community history can be meaningful without becoming a leaderboard.

## Long-term goal

The long-term goal is a durable independent community platform with:

- native accounts and profiles
- creative projects
- community groups
- forums
- activity and conversation
- events and challenges
- achievements and community history
- discovery tools
- accessibility and personalization
- optional supporter funding
- integrations with external services where useful

The platform should remain understandable enough that individual systems can be improved or replaced without requiring the entire project to be rebuilt.
