GATHER, MAKE, CELEBRATE

Events that make room for real life.

Shared creative seasons, flexible personal goals, and teams built around encouragement — not competition.

APRIL · REVISION & REFINEMENT

Make room to revise.

April is a season for editing, revisiting, reshaping, proofreading, and looking at a project with fresh eyes. Goals can be chapters, scenes, passes, sessions, structural notes, or a creative refresh.

NOVEMBER · DRAFTING & CREATION

Make room to begin.

November is a season for drafting and making new things. Members choose their own measure: words, scenes, sessions, minutes, days, chapters, or simply starting again. 50,000 words can be one goal — never the expectation.

Teams, together

Shared identity. No rivalries.

Each event can have its own teams. Participants are assigned to one team for that event, and teams have their own names, emblems, colors, banners, and gathering spaces. Team membership is about shared spirit and encouragement, not project ownership or a competition scoreboard.

Teams can celebrate check-ins, cheer each other on, and make the event feel like a shared occasion. There are no losing teams, defeat points, or public rankings.

A keepsake to carry forward

The event banner.

A personal banner remembers the event and the team a member belonged to. Its main field reflects the event theme; pixel-art keepsakes earned through participation are pinned across it.

Recognition, not ranking

Keep the good things.

Personal awards

Milestones can earn awards for trying something new, finishing a personal goal, returning to a project, participating, or helping someone else.

Community pennants

Collective keepsakes mark things teams or the whole community accomplish together. They belong to shared history.

The Milestone Gallery

A curated exhibition of awards and accomplishments, browsable by collection and event. No leaderboard and no ranking members against one another.

Progress belongs to you

Choose what progress means.

Goals can be words, minutes, sessions, scenes, chapters, revision passes, or other measures that make sense for a creative practice. Updates can be numbers or plain-language notes. Sharing is optional, and visibility can be private, team-only, or community-facing.

The community progress board is designed to show a mosaic of activity and possibility — never a ranked list of who's doing the most.
